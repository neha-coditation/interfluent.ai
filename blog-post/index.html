Everyone ships fast now. Together, you still don't.

AI made each function faster on its own. Cross-functional shipping got slower. What's happening in the handoffs, and why the fix is a shared model.

cover image

Walk into any product company this year and each room looks faster. Business drafts strategy with an assistant. Product turns a conversation into a PRD in an afternoon. Engineering merges forty changes a day. Measured inside the room, throughput is up everywhere.

Measured across the rooms, something odd shows up. The quarter's one cross-functional capability is still waiting on an estimate. The docs describe the system as it was in March. The security report lists files nobody in business can place on the roadmap.

The bottleneck moved

When producing an artefact was slow, the handoff was a small part of the cycle. Now the artefact is nearly free and the handoff is most of the cycle. Each room speaks its own language: capabilities, requirements, repositories. Translating between them was always manual. It just used to be hidden behind slower work.

More meetings don't fix this. A meeting is a one-time translation that is stale before the notes are typed up.

What a fix has to do

It has to hold capabilities, requirements, documentation and code in one model, so a business question and a technical question share an answer path. It has to price a change before a date is promised. And it has to rewrite the record when the system moves, holding the rewrite as a diff until a person accepts it.

The next change should be cheaper than the last. Today it is more expensive, because the map is redrawn by hand every time.

That is the part in between, and it is the part we're building. If you want to see it on your own codebase, book a walkthrough.
